## A baby, a toddler and a child

A baby turns a toy over in their hands. A toddler turns a lid. A child moves two counters to show how they worked something out.

These look like very different moments. In each, a child is trying something, looking at what happened, and changing how they try. That is the heart of learning to try a different way.

## What the adult does

In each case, the most useful thing an adult can do is the same: give another turn. Not a better method, not the answer, but another go.

When we hurry in, we often take away the very part that was teaching them.

## What to look for

Across the years from birth to eight, the signs change but the pattern stays the same. A baby shifts their grip. A toddler turns the lid before pressing. A six-year-old says, "Wait, I'll do it another way."

When you see a change between the first try and the second, you are watching a child learn.

## Where to begin

Pick one moment this week when your child gets stuck. Wait. Offer help with one part. Give them another turn, and look at what changes.
